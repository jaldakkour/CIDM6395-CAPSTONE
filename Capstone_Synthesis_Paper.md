# Synthesis Paper: Conversion Compass, Unifying Analytics, Management, and Systems

## Introduction

The Master of Science in Computer Information Systems and Business Analytics (MSCISBA) program is structured to develop a comprehensive understanding in four core topics in the MSCISBA program: Data Management, Software Development, Business Analytics, and Cybersecurity and Networking. The goal is that by the end of the program, students will have the ability to fuse technical and analytical knowledge areas into cohesive, high-value solutions. This capstone project, **Conversion Compass**, serves as the artifact proving this synthesis. The system addresses a critical data gap for small businesses: the inability to directly correlate social media engagement metrics with tangible sales revenues, resulting in strategic guesswork and an inability to calculate true Return on Investment (ROI).

The artifact I have created – Conversion Compass – solves this issue by creating an end-to-end pipline and a responsive analytical dashboard that unites social media and sales data. Its core feature is a predictive model that forecasts the impact of social media activity on sales. The analytics behind this artifact have the possibility in future to expand far beyond just analyzing social media impacts on sales, such as providing analytics on inventory, as well as predictive analytics on inventory sales, social media posts, and other such topics.

**Thesis Statement:** This paper argues that the Conversion Compass prototype successfully demonstrates a feasible synthetic understanding of the MS-CISBA foundational curriculum by effectively integrating concepts from **Data Management (DM)**, **Software Systems (SS)**, **Business Analytics (BA)**, and **Cybersecurity and Networking (CN)** into a functional, problem-solving artifact. The paper will clarify my foundational definition of each area, illustrate the process understanding between them, and substantiate these claims using my portfolio evolution material.

## Background

This section clarifies my definition of the foundational qualities of each curriculum area and provides evidence of my competency from my portfolio.

### Business Analytics (BA)

My definition of the foundational quality of **Business Analytics (BA)** is the systematic application of quantitative methods, statistical modelling, and data visualization to raw data sources, with the goal of extracting **actionable business insights** that will support **data-driven strategic decision-making**. This field focuses heavily on the end-to-end data processing lifecycle required to transform raw data into organizational value through predictive and prescriptive analytics. My coursework in Business Analytics was taught through the courses CIDM5310 Business Intelligence and Decision Support Systems (CIDM5310) and CIDM6308 Seminar in Data Analytics (CIDM6308). These courses emphasized strong theoretical foundations in retrieving and preparing data, statistical models, and understanding the strategic integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI). This proficiency is evidenced by the successful completion of the Prompt Engineering Certificate and the practical application of analytic tooling in the **Streamlit Homework Assignment** [2], alongside foundational visualization skills demonstrated in the **Tableau DataCamp Course** [3].

### Data Management (DM)

My definition of the foundational quality of **Data Management (DM)** is the systematic process of **collecting, organizing, storing, and maintaining high-quality data** to ensure its accuracy, reliability, and security. DM provides the essential groundwork for all data-driven activities by focusing on database design, implementation, and governance, transforming raw information into an accessible, structured asset. I see data management as the required first step in any function or process involving data. My competency is rooted in the structure and consistency of the **Structured Query Language (SQL)**, substantiated by the successful completion of **SQL coding homework assignments** completed in the courses CIDM6350 Data and Information Management (CIDM6350) and CIDM6355 Data Mining Methods (CIDM6355) [1a, 1b]. Furthermore, I developed confidence in data modeling using draw.io for **Entity Relationship Diagrams (ERDs)**. My proficiency in SQL is the bedrock upon which the Conversion Compass data model is built.

### Software Development (SD)

My definition of the foundational quality of **Software Development (SD)** is the **mastery of the Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC)**, focusing on procedural and object-oriented programming principles, application architecture (including **API design**), and the creation of **user-centric interfaces**. SD transforms conceptual requirements into functional, scalable, and maintainable applications that serve as the delivery mechanism for data and analytic insights. I consider data management to be the first step in any process concerning data, however **SS** is what I would say is the **“how”** or the methodology behind performing the data management aspect. I gained confidence in the design stages of the SDLC, evidenced by the **ERD Diagram** [6] and the **Software Description Paper** [7] detailing user stories and features. I also demonstrated skill in **user interface (UI) implementation** using **HTML/CSS** [1], [2]. The programmatic hurdle encountered in Python and Django REST Framework implementation (SS) underscores a key synthesis requirement: the need for strong DM and BA structure to guide and overcome complex SS implementation.

### Cybersecurity and Networking (CN)

My definition of the foundational quality of **Cybersecurity and Networking (CN)** is the **strategic implementation of network architecture and security policies** (e.g., risk management, governance, access control) to ensure the **confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA triad)** of data and systems. CN provides the protective layer and communication foundation upon which all SS applications, DM assets, and BA activities must securely operate. I developed confidence in fundamental network scanning and analysis, including the use of **NMAP** [1], **ShieldsUp** for port scanning [2], [6], and vulnerability scanners like **Nessus** [3]. These tools were used in the courses CIDM6340 Network Management and Security Information (CIDM6340) and CIDM6341 Cybersecurity and Risk Management (CIDM6341). My experience highlights the necessity of **Security by Design** to proactively protect systems, a concept that is actively being solidified through the CIDM6341 coursework.

## Illustrating Comprehensive Understanding Through the Process and the Artefact

This section illustrates a process understanding of the connections between these four areas and demonstrates how the Conversion Compass prototype constitutes a comprehensive understanding of the curriculum. The MS-CISBA areas function sequentially and symbiotically to create value.

Data management is the necessary first step in the process. The **Extract, Transform, and Load (ETL)** process prepares data for analysis by cleaning and structuring it, ensuring and maintaining data integrity. This allows for complex business analytics possible. In the Conversion Compass artifact, data management is implemented through a full, Python-based ETL pipeline which securely extracts social media data from Facebook and **normalizes it into a clean, time-series-ready SQL databases** that I built. Throughout this process, software development worked hand-in-hand with data management to perform the ETL process. Software development provides the delivery and architecture (or the means) for data management to work. The **Application Programming interface (API)** is the technical bridge that allowed for my database that I created to communicate with the social media site, Facebook, used to pull real social media data from. APIs are a vital software development tool that allow for applications to communicate to other applications, as well as databases, which is vital to data management through the extraction and load functions, as the cleaned, transformed data must be loaded into the analytical application. Business analytics is what I consider the end result. It is through the analytics part of the process that the end user will be able to identify the ROI gap, which is shown through **time-series predictive modelling**. Business analytics showcases the basic level analytics that are expected, such as showcasing social media engagement over time, sales data over time, and inventory over time. However, in Conversion Compass, business analytics works hand-in-hand with software development to generate and implement a **deeper level of time-series models** that forecasts sales based on social media activity, which inventory (Conversion Compass’ example data set is based off a tea room) such as tea flavours will sell best. This takes advanced analytical methods learned to provide the end user (the business owner) with **actionable intelligence** necessary to calculate ROI.

Lastly, while not one of the three out of four fundamental core areas required and showcased for the artifact, cybersecurity and networking principles are still important to remember and consider throughout the entire process. Cybersecurity and networking are vital for data management, as it is what helps to keep data secure, which assists in maintaining the availability and integrity of data and databases for business analytics. Cybersecurity plays a major role in software development as well, which can be seen in API development. Cybersecurity practices ensure that data is maintaining integrity as well as privacy when programs are communicating and sending data. Facebook’s API utilized security practices through access tokens. There were two types – a short-lived access token that only worked for a few hours, and a long-lived access token that will last for 90 days before expiring. This ensures that those who should not have access to the sensitive engagement data are not gaining access to it.

The project demonstrates that my knowledge of **SQL (DM)** and **ERDs (DM/SS)** was the necessary foundation to successfully implement the **API (SS)**, which then deployed the **predictive model (BA)**. This comprehensive approach proves a feasible synthetic understanding of the MS-CISBA curriculum.

## Conclusion

The Conversion Compass project stands as a definitive artifact of comprehension of all four areas. It directly addresses the technical challenge of disconnected data, proving the ability to define requirements (SS/DM), implement security (CN), process complex data (DM/BA), and deliver a functional product (SS). The project required overcoming previous weaknesses—specifically, leveraging the structure of DM and the theoretical understanding of BA to successfully build the programmatic components required by SS. This capstone experience demonstrates that the foundational knowledge areas are not just individual topics but are intrinsically linked, forming a unified, iterative process. The Conversion Compass is a technical solution that is also a strategic business tool, reflecting a true **synthesis** of the MS-CISBA program's core disciplines. A fully realized version of the artifact would further demonstrate this synthesis by evolving into a data-driven security dashboard that uses BA to proactively inform CN policy, built upon a robust and secure SS/DM foundation.
